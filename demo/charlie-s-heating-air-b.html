What the audit found on charlieshvac.com — and what the agenticrx version does about each one.

🟡 Leaving money on the table

The form asks for your street address before your problem

The 14-field form collects first name, last name, email, phone, street address, unit number — the customer describes their house before their broken AC. On mobile, in the heat, that order kills conversions.

The agenticrx fix: Conversational intake that asks “what’s wrong?” first — address comes after the customer is already helped.
🔴 Costing you jobs

No after-hours answer

No 24/7 claim, no emergency line, and no chat were found. New Orleans summer nights are brutal — but the site assumes every breakdown happens on schedule.

The agenticrx fix: Ray answers in 8 seconds, captures the job, and texts a confirmation — overnight, weekends, holidays.
🟡 Leaving money on the table

Two area codes, no guidance

Both 504-464-6103 and 985-651-5716 appear. The customer has to guess which one reaches a human — and guessing wrong costs the job.

The agenticrx fix: One primary tap-to-call number, tracked to the source — every call, text, and chat in a single inbox.
🟡 Leaving money on the table

No reviews on the site

No ratings, testimonials, or review badges were found. The customer is asked to hire on faith — after filling 14 fields.

The agenticrx fix: A live review feed on the homepage and texted review requests after every job.
🟡 Leaving money on the table

No booking, no text-back

No scheduling flow and no SMS path were found. The 14-field form is the only digital door — and it’s a heavy one.

The agenticrx fix: One-tap booking in chat plus missed-call text-back: the light doors every customer actually uses.
